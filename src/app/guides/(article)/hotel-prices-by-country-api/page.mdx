# Do hotel prices change by country?

<GuideDates published="2026-09-05" updated="2026-10-06" />

**Short answer:** Sometimes, and it depends on the hotel, the room and the day. FlightPowers'
Booking.com endpoints take a `proxy_country` field (a two-letter code) that sends the request
through a residential proxy in that market, so you can price a stay as a resident of that
country would see it. A 2026-08-28 run found Japan about 4% under Germany on three Rome
properties across repeat samples. A 2026-10-06 run on one of those properties found no gap
between Germany and Japan at all, and gaps of 3% to 16% between Germany and the US once the
comparison was made room by room.

Booking.com can quote a different rate depending on where the visitor appears to be browsing
from. Every hotels endpoint here takes `proxy_country`, a lowercase two-letter code; leave it
out and the request goes through the global pool. The mechanism is simple. Measuring what it
does is not, and this page is mostly about the measuring.

## How do you make the request?

Same call as any other lookup, one extra field. This is the exact request behind the
2026-10-06 tables below, with `de` swapped for `jp` and `us`:

```bash
curl -X POST "https://api.flightpowers.com/v1/hotels/by-name" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "x-api-key: $RAPIDAPI_KEY" \
  -d '{
    "hotel_name": "Suite della Pigna",
    "area": "Rome",
    "checkin_date": "2026-11-18",
    "checkout_date": "2026-11-21",
    "adults": 2,
    "currency": "EUR",
    "proxy_country": "de"
  }'
```

`proxy_country` is on the free plan too. Keys come from{' '}
<a href={rapidApiPricingUrl('hotels', 'guide')} rel="noopener">the RapidAPI listing</a>.

## What did the first controlled run show?

Three Rome properties, three markets, three identical requests per market through the search
endpoint. Only `proxy_country` changed. Captured **2026-08-28**, totals for the stay in the same
currency.

| Property | `de` | `jp` | `us` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Favola Romana Guest House | 243, 243, 243 | 234, 234, 234 | 242, 258, 258 |
| Suite della Pigna | 486, 486, 486 | 467, 467, 467 | 486, 523, 482 |
| Suites 51 | 477, 477, 477 | 460, 460, 460 | 477, 512, 473 |

Read the rows before the columns. Japan came back about 4% under Germany on all three
properties, and it held across three repeat runs. That is a real gap, and it is modest.

Then look at `us`. Three identical requests, seconds apart, returned 486, 523 and 482 on one
property. That spread inside one market is larger than the gap between markets. Sample `us`
once against a single `de` call and you would have "found" a 7.6% difference that is mostly
noise.

## What did the same check show five weeks later?

On **2026-10-06** I ran it again on one of those properties, Suite della Pigna, for a
three-night stay on 18 to 21 November 2026, two adults, in euros. This time through the
by-name endpoint, three samples per market, interleaved `de`, `jp`, `us` so no market got a
quiet minute to itself.

| Market | Sample 1 | Sample 2 | Sample 3 | Room the headline is for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `de` | €497 | €497 | €497 | Premium |
| `jp` | €497 | €497 | €497 | Premium |
| `us` | €573 | €573 | €573 | Deluxe Room |

Two findings, and the second matters more.

Germany and Japan were identical this time, on the same property where Japan ran about 4%
under Germany in August. A gap you measured once is a fact about the day you measured it, not
about the hotel.

The `us` rows look like a 15% gap and are not one. The headline rate is the cheapest room
Booking.com shows that market, and from `us` that was a Deluxe Room, not the Premium room the
other two markets got. €573 against €497 compares two different rooms. The `room_type` field
is in every answer for exactly this reason: check it before you subtract.

## How do you compare room to room?

With the rooms endpoint, which lists every room and rate plan for one property. I resolved the
name to its Booking.com ID once and then asked for the room list from `de` and from `us`, twice
each, a few seconds apart:

```bash
curl -X POST "https://api.flightpowers.com/v1/hotels/rooms" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "x-api-key: $RAPIDAPI_KEY" \
  -d '{"hotel_booking_id": "it/amazing-rooms-piazza-venezia", "checkin_date": "2026-11-18",
       "checkout_date": "2026-11-21", "adults": 2, "currency": "EUR", "proxy_country": "us"}'
```

The non-refundable rate for each room type, total for three nights:

| Room, non-refundable | `de` sample 1 | `de` sample 2 | `us` sample 1 | `us` sample 2 | `us` against `de` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium | €452 | €452 | not listed | €452 | level |
| Deluxe Room (cheapest) | €511 | €511 | €494 | €494 | 3% under |
| Loft - Separate Building | €735 | €735 | €615 | €615 | 16% under |
| Suite with Balcony | €924 | €924 | €773 | €773 | 16% under |

`de` listed two non-refundable Deluxe rates (€511 and €591) and `us` one (€494), so that row
compares the cheapest of each. The free-cancellation rates were lower from `us` as well, by
about 9% on each of the three rooms that had one.

So on this hotel, on this day, the market gap was not one number. It was zero on one room,
3% on another and 16% on two more, and it pointed the other way from the headline-rate table.
And `us` was again the least stable market: its first room list had no Premium room at all,
its second did.

One more thing you will notice: the room list priced the Premium room at €452 from `de`, while
the by-name call priced it at €497 from the same market. The two endpoints read two different
Booking.com pages, and with `proxy_country` set they did not print the same total for that
room (with no `proxy_country`, both said €497). So keep every comparison inside one endpoint.
A gap between a by-name number and a room-list number is not a market gap.

## How to measure it honestly

**Repeat every market.** One call per country is not a measurement, it is one draw from a
distribution you have not looked at. Sample each market several times and record the spread
next to the median.

**Hold the property fixed, then the room.** Compare one named property with itself across
markets, and then one room type and rate plan with itself. Comparing "the first result"
between markets compares different hotels, because result order changes between identical
runs. Comparing headline rates can compare different rooms, as the `us` row above did.

**Do not anchor on `us`.** It was the least stable market in both runs. Pick a market that
repeats cleanly, like `de` here, as your baseline.

**Date every claim.** "Japan ran about 4% under Germany on these three properties, on
2026-08-28, over three samples" is a claim you can defend. "Prices vary by up to 16% across
countries" is not, even though one row above shows 16%.

`price_as_seen_from` is an input parameter on the keyed hotels MCP server, the same idea as
`proxy_country`. It is not a field in the REST response, so do not go looking for it in the
JSON.

## What does a check like this cost?

Every call is one request. The 2026-10-06 run above was nine by-name lookups with
`proxy_country` (three markets, three samples), one without it, one resolve and five room
lists: 16 requests. A three-market, three-sample
check on one property fits inside the free plan's {HOTEL_PLANS[0].quota} a month, once. A
daily check on a comp set does not: ten properties, two markets, three samples is 60 requests
a day, about 1,800 a month, which is what PRO's {HOTEL_PLANS[1].quota.toLocaleString('en-US')}{' '}
for ${HOTEL_PLANS[1].priceMonthly} is sized for. Current plans always live on
[/pricing](/pricing).
