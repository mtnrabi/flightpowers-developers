# How to get round-trip flight prices in one request

<GuideDates published="2026-09-05" updated="2026-10-06" />

**Short answer:** FlightPowers' `POST /v1/flights/roundtrip` on `api.flightpowers.com` prices
the outbound and the return as one paired itinerary in a single call: one
`total_price`, one `buy_link`, and Google's own low / typical / high price band on the pair.
On a real run on 2026-10-06, Berlin to Paris and back, 17 to 24 November 2026, the cheapest
round trip was **$130**; the same two Air France flights searched as two one-ways came to
**$169**.

The obvious way to price a return trip is two one-way searches, one per direction, added
together. It is wrong in a way that does not show up until someone clicks through and sees a
different number. Airlines price a return as one product, so the sum of two one-way fares is
the price of two separate tickets, not of the round trip on the screen. Two searches also cost
you two requests for every date pair, which starts to matter the moment you scan a range.

This page runs both versions on the same route and dates, shows what came back, and covers
the parts of a round-trip response that trip people up.

## What did one request return against two?

All three searches below ran within a minute of each other on **2026-10-06** through
`api.flightpowers.com`, in US dollars, for Tuesday 17 November out and Tuesday 24 November
back. The flight numbers come from each result's `buy_link`.

| What was priced | Requests | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Round trip, cheapest pairing: Air France AF1535 out, AF1134 back | 1 | **$130**, one ticket |
| The same two flights as one-ways: AF1535 at $81 + AF1134 at $88 | 2 | **$169**, two tickets |
| The cheapest one-way each way: easyJet U2 5149 at $59 + AF1134 at $88 | 2 | **$147**, two tickets |

On this route, on this day, the round trip was $39 cheaper than buying the same two flights
as one-ways and $17 cheaper than the cheapest pair of one-ways, and it took half the requests.
That is one route at one moment, not a rule: on another route the two one-ways can come out
ahead. The point is that the two numbers are different products, so the number you show
has to be the one the user can buy, and a round trip is only priced by asking for a round
trip.

## How do you make one request for both dates?

```bash
curl -X POST "https://api.flightpowers.com/v1/flights/roundtrip" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "x-api-key: $RAPIDAPI_KEY" \
  -d '{
    "from_airport": "BER",
    "to_airport": "CDG",
    "departure_date": "2026-11-17",
    "return_date": "2026-11-24",
    "limit": 5,
    "currency": "usd"
  }'
```

The key is your RapidAPI key. The same call works on the RapidAPI host,
`google-flights-live-api.p.rapidapi.com`, with `x-rapidapi-key` and `x-rapidapi-host`
instead. A key comes from <a href={rapidApiPricingUrl('flights', 'guide')} rel="noopener">the
listing</a>, free plan included.

The answer came back as HTTP 200 with these headers:

```
x-search-status: ok
x-search-results: 5
x-search-combinations: 1
x-search-attempts: 1
```

`ok` with five results is a complete search; one combination is one date pair; one attempt
means nothing had to be retried.

One flat object per itinerary. Both legs are already matched, the price is the price of the
pair, and `buy_link` reopens that exact pairing on Google Flights, so the user lands on the
$130 fare and not on a search to redo. The band, $110 to $185 with a `typical` verdict, is
Google's judgement of the round-trip price for those dates, not of either leg alone.

The other four rows were $143, $157, $163 and $170, all Air France, all nonstop, all
`typical`. Decoding their links shows what they are: different pairings of three outbound
flights (AF1535 at 8:00 PM, AF1835 at 6:00 PM, AF1735 at 12:40 PM) with two return flights
(AF1134 at 8:55 PM, AF1434 at 7:15 AM). That is
what "paired" means in practice. The answer is a list of complete trips, each priced as
sold, not two lists for you to join.

## The trap that costs an afternoon

A round-trip result carries `total_price` and `total_price_as_number`. A one-way result
carries `price` and `price_as_number`. The two shapes are otherwise close enough that code
written for one-ways runs happily against round-trips and reads `undefined` for the price.

The insight fields keep their names across both, so the verdict still looks right while the
price is missing. That combination is how the bug survives review. Write the two paths
separately, and give each one a test against a saved real response like the one above.

## Which filters apply per leg?

The request takes separate settings for each direction, so "nonstop out, any return, back in
the morning" is one request, not a filter you run afterwards:

- `max_departure_stops` and `max_return_stops`
- `departure_airline_codes` and `return_airline_codes`, plus the `_exclude_` versions of each
- time windows per leg: `departure_departure_time_min` / `_max`,
  `departure_arrival_time_min` / `_max`, and the same four for `return_`

The common ones apply to the pair: `currency`, `max_price`, `seat_type`, `passengers`,
`sort_type` and `limit`. The full list is in the [OpenAPI spec](/openapi.json).

## How do other flight APIs handle a round trip?

Not all the same way, so check before you build on one. SerpApi and HasData need a second
search with a departure token to get the return leg priced. Ignav and flight-mcp.com also
price a round trip in one request. If you are moving from a two-step flow, the change is
mostly deletion: no token to carry from the first answer into the second, and no join.

## What does an empty response mean?

A paired search prices a return leg for every outbound candidate, and any of those inner
fetches can fail on its own. So an empty array can mean two very different things, and the
header tells you which. `x-search-status: ok` with no rows means Google had nothing for that
pair of dates. `degraded` or `partial` means some of the work was blocked or cut short,
which is not the same as "this route has no flights" and must never be stored as one. The
full taxonomy is in
[handling empty flight search results](/guides/handle-empty-flight-search-results).

## What does it cost to scan dates?

One request per date pair. A month of departure dates at a fixed seven-night stay is 30
requests as round trips and 60 as one-way pairs, and only the first gives you prices you can
show. PRO is ${FLIGHT_PLANS[1].priceMonthly} for{' '}
{FLIGHT_PLANS[1].quota.toLocaleString('en-US')} searches at{' '}
{FLIGHT_PLANS[1].ratePerMinute} a minute, so a 30-date scan fits in one burst. Current plans
always live on [/pricing](/pricing).

The endpoint takes one date pair per request, so a scan is your own loop over dates, sent in
parallel within the rate limit; [parallel date scans](/flights-api/parallel-date-scan) shows
the pattern.
